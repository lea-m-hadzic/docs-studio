**Reproducible sampling and aggregation for long-running measurement runs.**

`tidepool` records a measurement run as an append-only log, then replays it to produce
aggregates that are byte-identical on every machine. The log carries the sampling
decisions as well as the samples, so a result can be recomputed years later without the
instrument, the schedule, or the person who made it.

It is a bookkeeping tool, not an analysis library: it does not model, fit, or infer
anything. What it guarantees is that two people who replay the same log get the same
numbers.

```python
from tidepool import Run, mean

with Run.open("harbour-2026.log") as run:
    run.record(depth=4.2, salinity=31.1)
    run.record(depth=4.3, salinity=31.0)

summary = mean(Run.replay("harbour-2026.log"), field="salinity")
print(summary.value, summary.n)
```

!!! info "Replay is the only way to read a log"
    A log is never parsed in place. `Run.replay` walks it from the first entry, applies
    the recorded sampling decisions in order, and yields the samples that survive. Two
    replays of one log always yield the same sequence. See
    [Sampling and aggregation](aggregation.md#why-replay).

## What it gives you

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

-   **A log you can re-read**

    ---

    Every entry carries its timestamp, the schedule that admitted it, and a running
    checksum. A truncated log is detected on replay rather than quietly producing a
    shorter answer.
    See [Running a measurement](usage.md).

-   **Aggregates that do not drift**

    ---

    Aggregation is defined on the replayed sequence, in a fixed order, with no
    floating-point reduction that depends on how the work was split.
    See [Sampling and aggregation](aggregation.md).

## Scope

!!! warning "Not a storage backend"
    A log is a local file. `tidepool` does not replicate it, back it up, or coordinate
    writers across machines. One process writes one log at a time; concurrent writers
    are not detected and will interleave entries.

The on-disk format is versioned and readable by any later release, but it is not a
public interchange format — treat it as `tidepool`'s own file, not as a data format to
write from elsewhere.
