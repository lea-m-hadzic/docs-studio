**Lattice sums on periodic structures, with a convergence certificate.**

`Lattice.jl` evaluates slowly convergent sums over a periodic structure — the kind
where every term matters and the tail never quite goes away. It splits each sum into a
short-range part summed directly and a long-range part summed in reciprocal space, and
returns, alongside the value, a bound on everything it left out.

The bound is the point. A lattice sum truncated at some radius is a number with no
error bar; the same sum with a certificate is a number you can defend.

Developed by Example Lab. *This is an example site generated by `docs-studio`; the
package is fictional.*

[Quickstart](quickstart.md){ .md-button .md-button--primary }
[Installation](install.md){ .md-button }

```julia
using Lattice

structure = PeriodicStructure(
    cell = [1.0 0.0; 0.0 1.0],
    sites = [(0.0, 0.0), (0.5, 0.5)],
    charges = [1.0, -1.0],
)

result = latticesum(structure; splitting = 0.35, tolerance = 1e-10)

result.value, result.bound
```

!!! info "Sign convention"
    A site's contribution is positive when its charge has the same sign as the
    reference site's. Sums are taken over all images **except** the reference site
    itself, which is the self-term and is reported separately as `result.self`. See
    [Lattice sums](lattice-sums.md#the-self-term).

## What the splitting buys you

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

-   **Two fast sums instead of one slow one**

    ---

    The short-range part decays like a Gaussian and needs a handful of shells; the
    long-range part is smooth and needs a handful of reciprocal vectors. Neither needs
    the radius the direct sum would.
    See [Lattice sums](lattice-sums.md).

-   **A bound, not a hope**

    ---

    Both parts have tails that can be bounded above rather than estimated. Their sum is
    `result.bound`, and the solver chooses its cut-offs to drive it under the tolerance
    you asked for.
    See [The convergence certificate](certificate.md).

</div>

## Scope

!!! warning "Two dimensions and three, not one"
    The splitting as implemented assumes a periodic structure in two or three
    dimensions. A one-dimensional chain is a different problem — its reciprocal-space
    part does not converge the same way — and `PeriodicStructure` rejects a
    one-column cell rather than returning a number that looks plausible.

The package is not registered in Julia's General registry; it is installed from a clone
([Installation](install.md)).
